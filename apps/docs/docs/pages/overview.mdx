# Overview

0xSlots is a property primitive for scarce onchain assets.

It introduces **slots**: positions that are always priced and always
contestable. You set your own price, you pay tax on it continuously, and anyone
can buy it from you at that price whenever they like.

- Set it too high → the tax gets expensive.
- Set it too low → someone takes it.
- Set it honestly → you keep it.

There is no fourth option, and that is the whole design. Idle property becomes
costly to hold, and positions drift toward whoever actually values them.

The tax goes to a **recipient** fixed when the slot is created — a creator, a
community, a protocol, a public good. Scarce digital property stops being idle
control and becomes an ongoing revenue stream pointed at something.

## Why not just own it

The NFT era demonstrated what absolute ownership does to digital property. No
cost to hold and no pressure to use means the rational move is to hoard and
wait. Price mattered more than what the thing did; builders competed for
attention rather than utility.

The problem was never the technology. It was that ownership had no ongoing
obligation attached.

0xSlots is the middle ground — neither communal nor absolute. Ownership is
continuous, contestable, and tied to actually using the thing.

## What a slot is

A single contract, immutable by default, that:

- funds **one recipient**
- in **one currency**
- at **one tax rate**
- with at most **one utility module** and **one occupancy policy**

Want a different configuration? Deploy another. Slots are cheap and disposable
by design — there is no admin panel, because there is nothing to administer.

<Callout type="info">
Think [Splits](https://splits.org), but for collective ownership rather than
collective revenue.
</Callout>

## Slots are fertile ground

A slot starts empty. It is a position — nothing more.

What makes it valuable is what gets built on it. A **module** defines the rights
holding it grants: ad space, a name, access, publishing, revenue share.
**The slot is the plot; the module is the crop.**

An **occupancy policy** sets the terms of tenancy: whether you get guaranteed
time before you can be bought out, whether there is a price floor, whether a
queue decides who is next.

Each slot is a small, bounded space of productive ownership. Not a walled garden
you lock people out of — a public garden you tend. The market decides whether
you are tending it well enough to keep it.
